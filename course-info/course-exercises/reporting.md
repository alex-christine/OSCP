# Reporting

Report writing is often viewed as a necessary evil of PenTesting. As a result it is often not given the attention it deserves. Having a solid template can make reporting significantly easier.

[OffSec Reporting Template](https://www.offensive-security.com/reports/sample-penetration-testing-report.pdf)

## Important Focus Areas

The tester should focus on several key aspects of the engagement during the reporting phase.

### Objective

* What was the initial purpose of this engagement?&#x20;
* Were the objectives met?

Often PenTesters (especially junior ones) will get caught up in the technical aspects without considering the non-technical, business, or management objectives.

### Audience

* What does the audience need to learn?
* Who is the audience?
  * Are they technical or non-technical people?

Should include some sort of executive summary that can be easily digested by non-technical people.

It is safe to assume that the detailed findings will be handled by IT/Cybersecurity staff so those sections can be more detailed and technical.

### What To Include

More important than what to include is what **not** to include. Some things that **should not be included**:

* Copious amounts of tool output screenshots
  * Pages of irrelevant output make it difficult for readers to hone in on the important bits
* Screenshots should be used carefully in general
  * Screenshots should only be used to **demonstrate a point**, not just because something looks interesting
* Extra materials should be included as supplemental documents rather than in the main report
  * If there is material that drives up page count but will **only be interesting to a small sliver of the audience**, it should be provided as additional materials rather than included in the main report
* Information that is irrelevant to the objective
  * If hired to conduct a network test only but physical security flaws are found these flaws should not be included in the report
    * They could be included as a separate document finding&#x20;
